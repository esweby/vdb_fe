# Gate and sword worked hands

The later [Shard and Bloodfrenzy banish policy](bsl-banish-policy.md) records the maintainer's 25 Oct instruction: do not choose Shard for hand-banish effects, and prefer Bloodfrenzy outside the existing Descendancy setup. This is separate from the historical worked-line versions below.

These worked examples follow the guidance in [engine-calibration.md](engine-calibration.md#script-guidance-24-oct-2026). On 25 Oct the maintainer approved all five exact lines: Descendancy first, own permission without spending a Gate, an explicitly wanted traverse back, a spare-resource sword swing, and the spare-Shard / reserved-BSL hand. They later approved the bounded Descendancy extension described below. The lines use registered rules for the cards actually played. They are not a new default policy, a claim of optimal play or a change to the calibration baseline.

## Declared inputs

Each example starts from an **Assumed mid-turn position**, not a claim that an opening hand reached that position. Viserai is on Usurper, has one action point and has not attacked with Seven Sin Nebula this turn. An attack played in these examples is the turn's first blood debt attack, so Usurper gives it go again. A reserved card is chosen explicitly by the caller; the examples do not infer which card is a better starter.

The script fragments live in `apps/web/src/calibration/gateWeaponExamples.ts`. `runScriptFromState` runs the existing script interpreter from the declared state without shuffling a new hand or resetting the turn, resources or tokens. The fragments themselves do not enforce their written preconditions; use the guarded selector below rather than applying one to an arbitrary position.

The first example assumes starting life of 40 and five life lost, giving 35 life now. No more life is paid during that fragment: Descendancy is already banished. The maintainer changed the health requirement on 25 Oct to **over 17 life after paying**, replacing the earlier 25-life limits. `descendancyComfortableAfterPayment(life, payment)` and both Descendancy selectors share this check. Thus 21 minus 3 leaves 18 and passes; 20 minus 3 leaves 17 and fails. This is a policy guard, not a legality rule or an inferred danger assessment.

## Guarded selection

`selectGateWeaponExample(state, decisions)` in `apps/web/src/calibration/gateWeaponSelection.ts` returns a matching approved fragment or `undefined`. It does not change the input state. Before returning a fragment, it runs a trial with the existing interpreter and requires every step to complete. A failed swing therefore cannot leave a Gate spent or Shard pitched in the caller's state. The traverse-back fragment must also finish on Forsaken.

The selector is deliberately narrow, not a general playing policy:

- All matches need Usurper, the action phase, exactly one action point and an empty arsenal. Descendancy does not require a weapon or an unused Nebula. The other examples retain their weapon guards.
- The Bloodfrenzy examples still require the exact resource, Runechant, Gate and hand counts in the worked positions, exactly one face-up banished target of the reviewed pitch, and no earlier blood debt attack this turn. Their hands are empty.
- Descendancy uses the maintainer's bounded extension with explicit current life over 17 after any earlier payment, at least four Runechants, at least one Gate, blue Descendancy face up in banish as the first blood debt attack, and enough floating resources for Gate → Descendancy. With four Runechants that needs two resources; with five or more it needs one under the registered rules. Nebula is an optional follow-up, never an eligibility requirement. The selector includes it only if the attack is legal afterwards, and `nebulaAfterDescendancy: false` explicitly declines it. An absent, used or unaffordable weapon does not block Descendancy. No further life is paid and no card is pitched; the hand stays unchanged.
- Descendancy's play step explicitly uses `from: "banished"`, so an identical blue copy in hand cannot take its place. Scripted Gate selection skips face-down candidates rather than spending the Gate on a copy the line cannot play. Without a play-step source zone, the interpreter retains its existing hand-first order.
- Bloodfrenzy requires an explicit `traverseBack: true` or `false`. No momentum decision is inferred.
- Sword-only matches require `otherPlaysFinished: true` and a card played from banish in the current turn. A spare-resource match needs exactly one resource and either an empty hand or blue BSL with `reserveBsl: true`. The spare-Shard match needs exactly zero resources, only blue Shard and blue BSL in hand, and both `spareShard: true` and `reserveBsl: true`.

The caller can run a returned fragment with `runScriptFromState(state, example.script)`. Missing decisions or different hands do not gain a guessed fallback. The default script is unchanged. The opt-in batch connection is described below.

## Opt-in batches

The sixth approved line, `spare-shard-descendancy`, funds the Gate from the reviewed hand: Usurper, one action point, over 17 current life, zero resources, four Runechants, one Gate, face-up blue Descendancy as the first blood debt attack, and only blue Shard plus blue BSL in hand. The revised health guard replaces the original exact-25 restriction. With explicit `spareShard: true` and `reserveBsl: true`, pitch Shard → Gate → Descendancy from banish → Nebula. Five Runechants deal arcane damage; one resource remains, BSL stays in hand and no life is paid. Nebula may be omitted; then two resources remain. This is not permission to pitch any blue card or broaden the reviewed token or hand counts.

`runGateWeaponBatch(setup, options)` in `apps/web/src/calibration/gateWeaponBatch.ts` supports `baseline`, `gates`, `weapons` and `both`. It uses the same seed generator and report summaries as `runBatch`. With `baseline`, it returns the existing baseline report without adding policy metadata. No page or worker enables it by default.

**UNVERIFIED scheduling and ablation:** the lines are approved, but checking for them before each of the baseline's ten play attempts and before its end-turn step is an experimental batch policy. Gates-only enables the four Gate-related fragments without their Nebula swings. Weapons-only enables the two spare-resource or spare-Shard fragments, not a sword extracted from a line that first needs a Gate. Both enables the full fragments. Disabled fragments are filtered before selection so they cannot hide an allowed sword line. After the funded Descendancy line, remaining baseline play attempts are skipped to keep the explicitly reserved BSL, including in Gates-only mode. These are controlled comparisons, not separately verified optimal strategies.

The runner keeps the baseline's card choices, including explicit Cull and Scar answers. Missing answers still raise errors. A fragment that already ends the turn replaces that turn's remaining baseline attempts and end-turn step; it cannot end the turn twice. Every run records counts for all six fragments, including zero counts. The batch report keeps counts on failed runs separate from completed-run summaries; do not count those as successful matches.

The policy inputs now record `reviewed-gate-weapon-v5`, the mode and all supplied decision flags under **Assumed**. An optional explicit `startingLife` gives goldfish life as starting life minus the engine's self-loss. This assumes no incoming damage, prevention or life gain; it is not an observed opponent interaction. Without starting life, Descendancy's life guard does not match. No default life or reservation/traverse decisions are invented. Cull and Scar's fixed condition answers remain in the recorded script. Earlier population results do not measure the revised health requirement.

The original v1 selector required Open the Gate in hand, which the private deck does not contain. Its zero Descendancy matches are not evidence that Descendancy or Gates are unhelpful. Version 2 removed that restriction within the approved bounds; version 4 revises health only. Other targets, broader Bloodfrenzy hands, general pitch funding and exceptions at or below 17 life still need review. Historical results retain their original policy versions.

## Draft setup policy (25 Oct 2026)

`draft-descendancy-setup-v6` adds an opt-in **UNVERIFIED strategy**, separate from the approved examples above. It does not change the normal scenario or Goldfish policy.

- If blue BSL and blue Descendancy are available in hand, prioritise BSL and banish Descendancy. This can start on Forsaken. The choice uses the visible hand, not the next hidden cards.
- Pay the largest offered amount for Descendancy that leaves **at least 18 life**. Each payment reads self-loss at the moment it resolves, including earlier payments in the same action. For example, at 20 life the draft pays two, not three. Choosing partial payments this way is draft strategy, not a new card rule or an approved tactical judgement.
- On Usurper, try a face-up blue Descendancy as the first blood debt attack when a Gate and enough resources can be funded. Pitch available cards highest pitch first, in hand order for ties. Count Shard's extra Runechant when checking the cost. There is no exact two-card hand, four-Runechant minimum or empty-arsenal requirement for this draft line.
- `choices.keep` protects every matching card in hand from scripted play, automatic or explicit pitching, and hand-banish choices. It applies to the baseline steps too. The existing explicit `reserveBsl` decision protects blue BSL in non-baseline modes. Nothing infers which card should be kept.
- Nebula is optional. Both mode tries it with resources left after Descendancy; it does not pitch another card for the sword. Gates mode omits it. A missing, used or unaffordable sword does not undo the Gate line, and no on-hit Runechant is awarded.

Starting life is required for the draft. As before, it assumes no incoming damage or life gain. The 18-life floor constrains these optional payments, not subsequent blood debt loss or opponent damage. The draft does not assess danger, blocks, momentum or whether a different line is better. Existing approved examples still take precedence when their guards match. Historical v1–v5 results are unchanged.

The script format also accepts a bounded payment object, for example `lifePayment: { card: { name: "Arknight Descendancy", pitch: 3 }, startingLife: 40, minimumRemainingLife: 18 }`. It pays zero for another source. Setup preserves an explicit `lifePayment`, including zero or a stricter bounded policy; its automatic payment applies only when that setting is absent. Outside this draft setup, fixed numeric payments and the default zero are unchanged. Payment requests expose the resolving source and self-loss so the policy does not use a stale pre-action total. Hand-banish requests identify their source zone, so kept-card protection also covers cards drawn during the action.

See [the small seeded check](engine-calibration.md#draft-setup-sample-25-oct-2026) before planning a larger comparison. Match counts are not T5 measurements or proof of good play.

## What these lines do not establish

- **Open the Gate is not registered in the engine.** It is a held comparator in the first example and is not played or banished. The test does not compare a simulated Open-first line or add its missing rules. The original calibration deck still has 54 of 54 copies covered; that does not cover every card mentioned in strategic guidance.
- **Nebula makes a Runechant on hit, not just for attacking.** Hits are not resolved by this engine. None of these examples awards an on-hit Runechant or treats ending on one as guaranteed. A chance to end on one cannot yet be scored as a known outcome.
- **Life and danger are not inferred.** The comfort check requires explicit life and payment inputs. Exceptions at or below 17 life, an opponent's blocks and immediate danger are not implemented.
- **Retaining BSL and pitching Shard is not a general rule.** The maintainer approved that exact hand: Usurper, one action point, zero resources, unused Nebula, a card already played from banish, and blue Shard plus blue BSL in hand. The examples do not classify slower hands, choose pitch stacks or judge momentum.
- **Policy matches are not T5.** The opt-in [chain measurement](t5-chain-measurement.md) now counts one best BSL → Gate → blood debt attempt per run, matching the observed definition. The four fixed Cull/Scar cases and their thresholds are untouched. Formal T5 comparison still requires an approved Gate script; the v6 strategy remains unverified.
